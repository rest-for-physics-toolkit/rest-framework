 Finds all the peaks of a baseline subtracted signal given as (time, value) points sorted in time, e.g. TRestDetectorSignal::GetPointsVector. A point of the signal smoothed with a moving average of 2 * halfWindow + 1 consecutive points is a peak candidate if it is over threshold and at most maxGreaterEqual of the other points of that window are greater or equal. Points (bin, value) of the pulses over threshold of a baseline subtracted signal.

It returns a vector of the data points that are found over threshold. The parameters provided to this method are used to identify those points.

Parameters
rangeBins [first, second) searched; first < 0 becomes 0 and second <= 0 or beyond the signal becomes its size (the range is updated)
thrPar(pointThreshold, signalThreshold) in units of baseLineSigma: a pulse is a group of consecutive points over pointThreshold * baseLineSigma, kept if its standard deviation is over signalThreshold * baseLineSigma
nPointsOverMinimum number of points of a pulse
nPointsFlatA pulse ends after this number of consecutive points whose difference with the previous one is below the threshold (flat tails over threshold, e.g. overshoots)
baseLineSigmaBaseline fluctuation computed beforehand
thrParA std::pair defining two parameters: pointThreshold and signalThreshold. Both numbers define the number of sigmas over the baseline fluctuation, stored in baseLineSigma. The first parameter, pointThreshold, serves to identify if a single point is over threshold by satisfying the condition that is above the baseline by the number of sigmas given in pointThreshold. Once a certain number of consecutive points have been identified, the parameter signalThreshold will serve to reject the signals (consecutive points over threshold) that their standard deviation is lower that signalThreshold times the baseline fluctuation.
nPointsOverOnly data points with at least nPointsOver consecutive points will be considered.
nPointsFlatIt will serve to terminate the points over threshold identification in signals where we find an overshoot, being the baseline not returning to zero (or its original value) at the signal tail.
baseLineSigmavalue of the baseline fluctuation calculated beforehand
